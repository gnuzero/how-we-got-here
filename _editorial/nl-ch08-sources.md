## Sources opened and used

1. **V&A, Camera obscura, RPS.340-2017**
   https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1390376/camera-obscura-camera-obscura/
   Full object record opened. Supports ownership/use by Talbot; lens, mirror, ground-glass screen; tracing paper; movable inner box for focus. Used only for these object-level claims, **not** its sweeping “British inventor of photography” language.
2. **Harry Ransom Center, The Niépce Heliograph**
   https://www.hrc.utexas.edu/niepce-heliograph/
   Full institutional page opened. Dates the Le Gras image to 1827; calls it the earliest surviving camera-obscura photograph; describes bitumen on polished pewter, several days' exposure, the scene, heliography, 1829 partnership and 1839 introduction by Daguerre. The article explicitly attributes date/priority to the holding institution, rather than silently adjudicating alternative historical dates.
3. **STAM, Daguerreotypie van de Predikherenlei en -brug in Gent, object 00816**
   https://stamgent.be/en/collection/artworks/00816
   Full Dutch collection text opened at this English-prefixed URL. Supports François Braga and Joseph Pelizzaro, 22 October 1839, window at the Ajuinlei, view of bridge/quay, one of the earliest Belgian photographs, direct unique image on silvered copper. No claim that this was Belgium's absolute first photograph; no unnecessary biography or migration dates repeated.
4. **National Science and Media Museum, The history of photography in pictures, Talbot section**
   https://www.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/objects-and-stories/history-photography
   Full page opened; Talbot section used. Supports early 1834 negative/positive method and contact printing, August 1835 Lacock window negative, and September 1840 invisible/latent image after short exposure followed by chemical development. Avoids identifying the 1834/1835 work as the later calotype.
5. **History of Science Museum, Oxford, Calotypes**
   https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/features/ephotos/ctypes.html
   Full page opened. Explicit: invented September 1840, patented 8 February 1841. Supports paper negative, silver iodide sensitive layer, development and separate positive print. Article is a conceptual explanation, not a recipe. No precise exposure-time boast or unlimited-copy claim.
6. **Rijksmuseum, Portret van Charlotte Asser, dochter van de fotograaf, RP-F-AB12282**
   https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/nl/collectie/object/Portret-van-Charlotte-Asser-dochter-van-de-fotograaf--b0330a1d299f1e1c958fb05919de653e
   Full Dutch object record opened. Supports Eduard Isaac Asser, daughter Charlotte, daguerreotype, circa 1842, one of the earliest surviving Dutch examples. Date remains approximate. Museum's persistent identifier is https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200412052 ; the citation uses the record actually opened, not an untested replacement URL.
7. **Rijksmuseum, Uitzicht vanuit het raam van Hippolyte Fizeau in Parijs, RP-F-2022-92**
   https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/nl/collectie/object/Uitzicht-vanuit-het-raam-van-Hippolyte-Fizeau-in-Parijs--784b20d8d90f5ab8189188875c37688f
   Full object record opened. Supports the account of Fizeau experimenting with etching a daguerreotype plate and printing in ink. Used to qualify simplistic “daguerreotypes could never be copied” claims. No claim that the catalogued object is certainly by Fizeau (record says attributed); no technical recipe or object-material contradiction propagated.

## Lucas: check these claims first

- **1840 discovery / 1841 patent.** Both museum accounts give September 1840 for the breakthrough; Oxford explicitly gives 8 February 1841 for the patent. Original patent specification not collated. Do not move the discovery into 1841 merely because that is the patent year.
- **No exact discovery day.** Search results exposed differing precise September dates; the article deliberately uses September 1840 only. A Bodleian catalogue fetch displayed an anti-bot challenge; no challenge was solved and that page is **not** a citation or a verified primary document here.
- **Calotype versus earlier photogenic drawing.** Oxford describes the calotype sensitive layer as silver iodide. At this base, EN says silver chloride in its calotype paragraph. NL does not repeat that conflation; Lucas should consider the chemistry wording alongside the requested EN dating correction. EN remains untouched.
- **Niépce dating and priority.** 1827 / earliest surviving camera-obscura photograph is explicitly HRC's attribution. “Several days” stays approximate; no asserted eight-hour exposure, no claim that 1839 is the first exposure ever.
- **Gent object 00816.** 22 October 1839, makers Braga/Pelizzaro and Ajuinlei viewpoint follow STAM. “Among the earliest” stays qualified. The linked page is Dutch despite `/en/` in its URL.
- **Asser object.** “Circa 1842” and “one of the earliest surviving Dutch daguerreotypes” follow Rijksmuseum; not a dated national invention/priority claim.
- **Copying distinction.** A direct unique plate is not a paper negative; this does not make all reproduction impossible. Rijksmuseum's Fizeau account supplies the qualification. The paper model illustrates a workflow only, not chemical imaging.
