    Credits, Sources, Attribution

    Credits & Doors Out#

    This journey names constraints and people in the chapters. When a topic already has a strong primary, we point out. Wikipedia is a map, not the destination. The Chinese, Thai, Sinhala, and Tamil desks credit published local literature where it exists, and leave a gap open rather than fill it with a guess.

    Team roles (concept, writing, desks) live on About. This page is the professional record before the Foreword, so no one has to guess who already published this material.

    Standards and primary specs#

    Living, numbered specs the spine already leans on. One home each — not a catalog of every standards body.

    • RFC Editor and the IETF — the Request for Comments series (Jon Postel edited it for nearly three decades). Door-outs already in the chapters include RFC 1, RFC 791, RFC 793, RFC 1034, RFC 1035, RFC 1459, RFC 1591, RFC 6749, RFC 8446.
    • IANA — protocol parameters and number registries.
    • ICANN — names and numbers policy seat.
    • Unicode Consortium — shared character repertoire (The Unicode Standard).
    • IEEE — link-layer families such as IEEE 802 (Ethernet, Wi-Fi).
    • W3C and WHATWG — web platform meaning.
    • PCI-SIG — PCI Express Base Specification.
    • RISC-V International — the open ISA manual.
    • Free Software Foundation — GNU GPL, the license lineage named in the Unix/Linux chapters.

    Chapter door-outs (Baran, Tomasulo, Kung/Leiserson, and the rest) stay in those chapters. This page does not reprint them.

    Reference works#

    Wikipedia and its language editions are orientation maps. Verify against a primary before treating a page as settled. We link out; we do not host encyclopedia text.

    Wikimedia Commons is the image door (see below). Language editions (中文, ไทย, සිංහල, தமிழ்) are the same kind of map for those readers.

    Thai computing and encoding#

    The textbooks above are chapter อ่านเพิ่ม. This is the localization shelf that was missing — who made Thai work on computers.

    • ศาสตราจารย์ บัณฑิต กันตะบุตร (Bundhit Kantabutra) — Thailand’s first digital computer, an IBM 1620, at Chulalongkorn Statistics, 9 August 1963. Numbers and Latin only; Thai script came later. — Chula archive
    • IBM Thai on card-punch and line printers from the late 1960s used 8-bit EBCDIC (later remembered as CP838). Individual code-page designers are not publicly credited. — Koanantakool, Karoonboonyanan, and Wutiwiwatchai, Computers and the Thai Language, IEEE Annals of the History of Computing 31(1):46–61, 2009, citing Hensch 2005 — DOI
    • ยืน ภู่วรวรรณ (Yuen Poovarawan, Kasetsart; IEEE spelling Poovorawan) — first interactive Thai microcomputer text editor (1981); Thai Easy Writer (1982); Thai Kernel System. — IEEE Annals 2009; NECTEC MLIT97 timeline. Widely reported as TIS 620 vice-chair; we do not have a TISI roster in hand.
    • TISI — TIS 620-2529 (1986) Thai character codes; TIS 620-2533 (1990) same code points, clearer text; TIS 820-2531 (1988) keyboard (Ketmanee, one change). Unicode Thai (U+0E00–U+0E7F) and ISO/IEC 8859-11 follow TIS 620 in visual/typing order. — IEEE Annals 2009 Table 1; TIS 620
    • ดร. ทวีศักดิ์ กออนันตกูล และคณะ / TAPIC — คอมพิวเตอร์กับภาษาไทย, NECTEC, 10 Oct 2534/1991 (WTT 2.0). Later TIS 1566-2541 (1998). — NECTEC
    • Hugh Thaweesak Koanantakool, Theppitak Karoonboonyanan, and Chai Wutiwiwatchai — Computers and the Thai Language, IEEE Annals 31(1):46–61, 2009 — DOI
    • เทพพิทักษ์ การุณบุญญานันท์ (Theppitak Karoonboonyanan) — Thai locale, collation, libthai / linux.thai.net. Named as 2009 co-author and in the NECTEC WTT note.
    • Trin Tantsetthi — IANA TIS-620 charset registration, 25 Aug 1998; IEEE 2009 credits him for ISO-IR-166 and keeping visual order in the Unicode Thai block. — IANA request
    • Ketmanee vs Pattajoti: inherited typewriter layouts. IEEE 2009 names Pattajoti (1966; official, then abandoned 1971) and says Ketmanee is “named after its inventor” without a locked personal identity. We do not credit สุวรรณประเสริฐ เกษมณี as fact.

    A voice we can hear but cannot yet name#

    On 10 September 2026, Raz & Upgrade released Zunara on Regroup Records. You can hear a woman singing in Sinhala. The store page names the producer and the label; the singer’s name is not listed there.

    We mention it because this page is about putting names on the line where we can. Hearing a voice does not tell us whose it is, so the line stays open for now.

    What we can write:

    • Track: Zunara — Raz & Upgrade (Raz Kfir), © 2026 Regroup Records. Official player above — Spotify’s license with the label, not a file we host.
    • Language of the refrain: Sinhala. A gloss, not a lyric reprint: someone hiding among the stars, hiding in a dream, left alone, asking when they will come.

    What we cannot write yet:

    • The vocalist’s name. It is not given in the sources available to us, so we leave it blank rather than guess.
    • A Creative Commons mark on the recording, which is not released under CC.
    • A hosted stem or rip. An isolated vocal is a derivative of a © 2026 master. It stays outside this CC BY 4.0 book.

    If the singer or the label publishes a credit, we will add the name here, with thanks.
