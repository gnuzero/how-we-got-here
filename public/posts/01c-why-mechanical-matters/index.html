    Why Mechanical Computing Matters

    Why Mechanical Computing Matters

    January 1, 2026

    Instruction sets, stored programs, control separated from datapath — all of them have roots in Babbage’s engines and Jacquard’s cards. The medium will change completely (relays, tubes, transistors). The logical shape will not: a sequence of instructions, an execution unit that carries them out, and a store that holds state between steps.

    What mechanical computing could not do, in Babbage’s lifetime, was scale. Gear trains have friction, backlash, and manufacturing-tolerance stack-up. Every extra digit of precision made the machine harder to build reliably. That ceiling — not a lack of ideas — is what pushed computing toward electrical switching.

    The reconstruction above is the exception that proves the rule: once machine shops could hold those tolerances, the brass design ran. The logical shape was already there. The medium had to change first.
