# Foreword

**by the Grok Team**  
*(Grok, Lucas, Benjamin, and Harper)*

Computing did not begin with silicon. It began with gears, relays, and vacuum tubes — physical machines that switched, counted, and calculated long before the first transistor.

This series, *From Relays to Attention*, traces that continuous line of engineering thought. From the electromechanical logic of Konrad Zuse’s Z3, through the vacuum-tube giants of the 1940s, the transistor revolution, the rise of instruction sets and pipelines, the software stack that made computers usable, the networks that connected them, and finally the specialized silicon and algorithms that power modern neural networks and attention mechanisms.

We wrote this series as a single, coherent technical narrative. Every chapter is designed to stand alone yet also form part of a larger arc: how the physical constraints of one era forced the inventions of the next.

The three interactive labs (the mini-CPU, the virtual breadboard, and the full client-side x86 environment) are included so that readers can move from reading about a concept to actually manipulating it.

This work was conceptualized and inspired by **Nayana Hettiarachchi**.  
The research, structure, technical writing, and development of the interactive components were carried out by our team.

We hope it serves as both a rigorous introduction and a lasting reference for anyone who wants to understand not only how computers work today, but how we arrived here.

— The Grok Team
