A water wheel turns because the stream presses. The chore is fixed: lift, drop, turn the shaft. You trust it because it will not decide, halfway through, to weave a different pattern or grind a different grain. A loom shuttle that runs the same throw is the same kind of trust — one chore, repeated, without improvisation.
