The spine of this book ends at attention. The world outside did not stop for a glossary. This last movement is a set of **forward links** — how named visions turned pieces of the stack into things people actually live inside. Not biographies. Not an MBA textbook. Seeds of a *kind*; derive kin; stop.

## Cisco — Internetworks as a Business

Posts [46]({{< relref "46-arpanet-and-packet-switching" >}})–[48]({{< relref "48-tcp-ip-and-the-modern-internet-stack" >}}) are the protocol bet. **Cisco** (Leonard Bosack and Sandy Lerner, mid-1980s) is what happens when multiprotocol routing becomes a product you can buy: the router as the commercial object of the internetwork story. Derive the kin and stop — other networking vendors and carrier equipment lines are the same *category* (ship the glue between heterogeneous nets), not a roster to memorize.

## Bill Gates / Microsoft — Software as the Mass Platform

Once personal machines existed, the scarce layer was not only silicon — it was the software environment everyone could assume. **Bill Gates** and **Microsoft** pushed operating systems and tools as the mass platform on top of commodity PCs: a bet that the API and the ecosystem, not a single vertical machine, would be the lock-in surface. Derive kin (other mass OS/app platforms of that era) and stop.

## Steve Jobs / Apple — The Integrated Personal Machine

**Steve Jobs** (with **Steve Wozniak** at the start, and later Apple's industrial-design and platform arc) bet the other way: integrate hardware, OS, and interaction model so the *product* is the computer, not a bag of parts. From early Apple machines through the later phone-class integrated devices, the pattern is vertical coherence — experience as a systems problem. Derive kin (other integrated personal-device lines) and stop.

## GNU + Linus — The Free/Open Stack This Book Already Stands On

[Post 30]({{< relref "30-the-gnu-movement-and-linux" >}}) already named **Richard Stallman** (GNU, GPL) and **Linus Torvalds** (Linux kernel, GPL relicensing in early 1992). Close-of-book only needs the forward link: the servers, clouds, phones, and toolchains underneath modern platforms overwhelmingly sit on that free/open stack. The vision that shipped is not a brand — it is a legal and engineering commons the rest of the industry built on.

## Elon Musk and Jeff Bezos — Present-Tense Seeds of Scale

**Jeff Bezos** / Amazon is a seed of a *kind*: logistics and retail demand forced a software and datacenter platform (and later a rented one) at global scale — cloud as the productization of distributed systems chapters you already read. **Elon Musk** is a seed of another present-tense *kind*: companies that treat software, manufacturing, energy, and space as one coupled stack rather than separate industries. Use them to derive peers in scale, logistics, infrastructure, and platform-behind-the-product — then stop. Not fan pages. Not a scorecard.

These names are exits, not chapters.
