Print made a page you could copy without a scribe. But a press still moved ink that a person had cut, set or drawn. Photography was the first time a machine let light make the picture by itself, and that is a bigger jump than anything else on this spine so far.

## The first uncertain step

The earliest surviving photograph made in a camera obscura is a view from a window at Le Gras, made in 1827 by Joseph Nicéphore Niépce. He coated a polished pewter plate with light-sensitive bitumen, set it in a camera near the window of his upstairs workroom, and left it for several days. The plate kept an image of the courtyard, outbuildings and trees. Niépce called the process *héliographie*, "sun writing", and in December 1827 described it as "the first uncertain step in a completely new direction." ([Harry Ransom Center](https://www.hrc.utexas.edu/niepce-heliograph/))

## 1839: one image, or many

In 1829 Niépce went into partnership with Louis-Jacques-Mandé Daguerre. Daguerre kept improving the method after Niépce died and introduced the daguerreotype in 1839, the year the invention of photography was announced in both France and England. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://www.hrc.utexas.edu/niepce-heliograph/))

In England, William Henry Fox Talbot's calotype took a different route. Paper coated with silver chloride was exposed in the camera, giving a negative. Talbot found that gallic acid could "develop" the image, cutting exposure from about an hour to about a minute. One paper negative could then print any number of positives, while a daguerreotype was a single image on metal that could not be duplicated. Talbot patented the process in 1841. ([Britannica, "calotype"](https://www.britannica.com/technology/calotype))

## Why it sits here

The pattern is the same one this book keeps finding. The daguerreotype was a one-off, like a hand-built engine. The calotype negative was a master that could be copied, like a carved block or a punched card. Once light could be stored and copied, the next step was to store it in sequence and play it back. That story, from Muybridge's horse to the first film screenings, and China's first film, is told in [From Press to Pixel]({{< relref "51-from-press-to-pixel" >}}).
